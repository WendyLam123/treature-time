A pretty honest breakdown of Millennium Hall from a current resident

Quick Facts

Address

223 N 34th St, Philadelphia, PA

Cost

About $4,015 per term

Style

Hybrid hall (private bathroom stalls per room/floor setup)

Floors

17 total — 17th floor has a sky lounge with a great view

Overview

Millennium is one of the more modern Drexel dorms. It looks clean outside, but inside it’s very concrete-heavy and minimal. Some people like it, some think it feels a bit cold — it really depends on your vibe.

The best part is definitely the views. The 17th floor lounge is one of the nicest spots on campus, especially at night.

Building Layout

The building has 17 floors. Floors 2–16 are standard dorm floors. Floor 1 is common space, and floor 17 is split between rooms and the sky lounge.

Room numbers follow a floor + room pattern. The layout is slightly curved, so not every room faces the same exact direction.

One thing people don’t expect: the concrete structure can mess with cell service depending on your room location.

Rooms

Most rooms are doubles. They’re not tiny, but they’re not spacious either.

When you move in, you get the basics:

  • Two Twin XL beds
  • Two desks + chairs
  • Two dressers
  • Closet space

Beds are adjustable, but lowering them is annoying and usually takes more than one person.

Bathrooms & Kitchens

Bathrooms are private single-use rooms, which is honestly one of the better features here.

Each floor also has a shared kitchen and two lounges — one for studying and one more social.

Kitchens don’t include microwaves or dishwashers, and ovens take a while to heat up, so you kind of have to plan ahead.

Common Areas

Each floor has two lounges. They’re fine — nothing fancy — but good enough for studying or hanging out in small groups.

Outlets are limited, so most people end up using extension cords or going back to their rooms.

Laundry, Mail, Internet

Laundry is free and located on the first floor. It can get crowded during peak times.

Wi-Fi is solid, and Ethernet is very fast if you use it.

Mail is probably the most annoying part — pickup times are limited and packages can take a while to process.

Final Thoughts

Millennium is a middle-ground dorm. Not super social, but quieter and cleaner than a lot of freshman housing.

Biggest pros are the view, privacy, and modern feel. Downsides are smaller rooms and less social energy compared to places like Towers or Kelly.

Overall: solid choice if you want something calmer but still central to campus.
