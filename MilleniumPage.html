The Specs

What you're signing up for

Address

223 N 34th St, Philadelphia, PA 19104

Cost

About $4,015 per term

The Setup

17 floors of double-occupancy rooms radiating out from a central core.

Elevators

There are three, but they're slow. Give yourself an extra 5 minutes to get to class.

First Impressions

Millennium is the big cylindrical tower you see from basically anywhere on campus. From the outside, it looks super modern, but once you move in, it’s a lot of gray concrete and metal. It isn't for everyone—some people think it feels a bit like a lab—but it's definitely one of the cleaner spots for freshmen.

The View: If you're lucky enough to be on a high floor facing the city, the view is the best part of living here.
The Bathrooms: You don't have to deal with the giant "communal" shower vibe. Every floor has private-entry bathroom and shower stalls.

Dealing with the "Pinwheel" Layout

Because of the building's shape, the rooms are basically triangles that narrow toward the door. They are officially the smallest rooms on campus, so arranging your stuff is a bit of a nightmare at first. You’ll spend the first week playing Tetris with your desk and bed to make everything fit.

The Resident Reality

The "Millennium Shake" is real. When it’s windy, you can feel the building sway a little if you’re on the higher floors. It’s safe, but it's weird to get used to. Also, the 17th floor sky lounge is the only place to study when the library is packed during finals week.

Practical Details

  • Kitchens: There's a fridge and stove on every floor, but they're pretty basic. Don't leave your stuff in the fridge too long.
  • The Walls: They’re concrete, so you won't hear your neighbor's music, but you might lose cell service if you're in the wrong corner of your room.
  • Laundry: It’s on the first floor. Don't even bother trying to do it on Sunday night—it's always packed.

This guide is based on what students actually tell us, not official University brochures.
